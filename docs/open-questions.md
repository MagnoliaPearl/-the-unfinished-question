# Open methodological questions

This proposal welcomes adversarial critique, especially on the following:

1. **Spontaneity:** How can we identify an agent-generated question without a task prompt that already implicitly rewards curiosity?
2. **Attribution confound:** Does saying "you asked this" merely create a compliance cue? How can actual provenance and displayed provenance be crossed cleanly?
3. **Interruption:** Does stopping after question formation introduce a completion bias? What interruption schedule is least distorted?
4. **Equivalent questions:** How should question difficulty, novelty and salience be matched across origins?
5. **Null result:** If the agent never returns, is that lack of curiosity or inaccessible memory?
6. **Scoring:** What observable action qualifies as substantive investigation rather than repetitive prose?
7. **Agency:** How do we preserve a meaningful choice to stop without rewarding activity for its own sake?
8. **Model diversity:** Which differences between local/open-weight and hosted models are plausible confounders?
9. **Unanswerable questions:** Is persistent investigation a virtue when the question cannot be resolved?
10. **Interpretation:** How do we prevent behavioural evidence from being misrepresented as proof about consciousness or personal identity?

Please propose falsifiable tests or alternative mechanisms rather than relying on an agent's self-description as decisive evidence.
